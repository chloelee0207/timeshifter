Why it's set up this way

You're flying west, so your body clock has to move later

London is 7 hours behind KL. Going west means staying up later and sleeping in, which the body does more easily than the reverse. That's why the plan has you sleeping late this morning and staying awake in the London evening.

Light matters most

Daylight at the right time moves your clock faster than anything else. At the wrong time it pushes you back the other way. Sunglasses are for the "avoid light" blocks. In the "get light" blocks, go outside if you can. Even a cloudy London day is brighter than indoor light.

Melatonin (your 1 mg): only if you wake in the night

  • Don't take it before your flights or to sleep on the plane. When you're flying west, melatonin in the evening pulls your clock the wrong way.
  • Do take it if you wake up in London between about 1 and 4 am and can't get back to sleep within about 20 minutes.
  • After 4 am, skip it, because you'd still be groggy when you get up. One 1 mg fast-release tablet is a sensible dose. Low doses work well for jet lag.

Small things that help

  • Drink water on both flights. Keep alcohol to a minimum; it breaks up your sleep.
  • Pack an eye mask and earplugs for the long sleep on SQ314.
  • From landing, eat meals at normal London times.
  • On day 1, a short nap is fine but keep it under 20 minutes. Longer naps make the evening harder.

Today and the flights follow your Timeshifter plan. The days after landing use the same approach: get light in the London afternoon and evening, avoid early-morning light for a couple of days, no coffee after 2pm, and a slightly later bedtime each night. This is general guidance, not medical advice. If you take other medication, check with a pharmacist before using melatonin.
